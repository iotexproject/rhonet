Put your GPU on a bounty that has been open since 1997.

Certicom's ECCp-131 challenge pays $20,000 to whoever computes one discrete logarithm. It takes about 6.5 × 1019 elliptic-curve steps, roughly three weeks of a thousand consumer GPUs. RhoNet is the pool that lets strangers do it together: every verified step earns credit, and when two walks collide the prize splits pro rata.

Join

three commands

Your machine mints its own admission, then starts walking.

A ticket is real curve work, so a botnet gets no advantage over an honest GPU. Credits accrue to the address you name and survive you switching the machine off.

Payment

work in, credit out
  1. You walk the curve.

    Your GPU follows a pseudo-random path from a start point derived from your own public key. Occasionally it lands on a distinguished point, one whose x-coordinate ends in enough zero bits. That is the only thing you ever send.

  2. Each point is worth its expected work.

    A point with w trailing zero bits stands for 2^w steps, and one credit is 2^30 verified steps. No block reward, no halving, no treasury, no vote.

  3. A sample of your work is replayed.

    After each epoch closes, some of your segments are walked again from scratch. Which ones is decided by entropy that does not exist while you are submitting, so there is nothing to aim at. One failure forfeits everything.

  4. Your balance is committed on chain.

    Every epoch the coordinator posts one Merkle root of every address and its credited steps to a vault on an Ethereum L2, built from balances that already survived that epoch's audit.

  5. The collision ends the round and pays everyone.

    When two walks meet, the discrete logarithm falls out. The vault verifies it on chain itself, then each contributor sends one transaction and pulls their share. A prize that arrives weeks later is still claimable under the same root.

Verification

why a stranger should believe any of this

The prize is locked before the first step

Funds sit in a contract, not an organizer's account. Abort is a condition, not a decision: a deadline, coordinator silence, or somebody else solving it first, each provable on chain, each callable by anyone, and each sponsor recovers their own deposit.

The coordinator issues no work

Start points come from the round and your public key, so anyone can recompute anyone's walk from public data. There is no secret assignment to trust and no seed server to fail.

The audit is published, not just performed

Each epoch commits to a random beacon before it opens and reveals it after auditing, together with exactly which segments were replayed. A third party can re-derive the audit and check it was not steered.

The chain checks the answer

Settlement is gated on an on-chain reveal that verifies the discrete logarithm against the round's curve. The vault does not take the operator's word that the round was won.

Sampling beats proving

Proving every elliptic-curve step in zero knowledge costs orders of magnitude more than the work it proves. Replaying an unpredictable sample, and forfeiting on failure, is the honest verifier for this problem.

The failures are public too

An external review found four critical and six high-severity defects. A second, adversarial review of the repair found more. Every one, with its reproduction and its fix, is in the repository.

Calibration

1,200 solves, not one anecdote

A single solve proves almost nothing: rho's completion count has a standard deviation near half its mean, so a round ending at 40% or at 200% of expectation is unremarkable. So we solved 300 independent instances at each of four sizes and checked every answer against the generator's secret.

Theory puts the constant at 1.25 for this walk. The fitted scaling exponent is 0.5000 with R² = 0.9998: cost is square-root in the group size, with the constant where it belongs. Before the walk's branch function was decorrelated from the distinguished-point test, every distinguished point took the same branch out of 128 — a defect that would have surfaced only as a slightly wrong exponent.

Status

read this before sending anyone money
Demonstrator. Nothing here should hold funds yet.

The protocol runs end to end on toy curves and the settlement contract has been rewritten twice under review, but no public round has been opened and no prize has ever been settled on chain. Before ECCp-131 can start we need a GPU kernel for the 131-bit field and a measurement of its real throughput, which is the number the entire schedule above depends on. Findings, reproductions and fixes are tracked in the open at review/2026-09-08-external-review.md.
